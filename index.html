You decide what counts.

Enthusiasts don’t agree on what a credit is. Racing sides, re-tracks, the kiddie one you rode to keep someone company — everybody draws the line somewhere different.

Coastory doesn’t draw it for you. It records what you rode, exactly as it happened, and leaves the counting to you.

“Anywhere from 1 to 2 — your count, your rules.” Dawson Duel has two sides. Ride both and Coastory keeps both — then lets you say whether that’s one credit or two.

What you actually do with it

Rate it, rank it, remember why

Score a ride on your own scale and keep a note in your own words. Every coaster carries its own almanac too — height, drop, speed, model, and how it came to be.

Remember the day, not just the count

Start an Adventure when you walk into the park. Log rides as they happen, tag how each one felt, add the photos and the moments, then wrap the day up into something you keep.

See how far you’ve been

Every country you’ve ridden in, lit up. Spin it, zoom in, and tap a country to go back through the parks you visited there.

Why Coastory

Facts stay facts

What you rode and when is recorded exactly as it happened. What it’s worth is a separate question, and that one is yours.

Everything is optional

Ratings, rankings, tiers, statistics — use the ones you care about and ignore the rest. A park is fine untiered.

Your photos stay on your phone

Coastory never uploads your photos. Back them up yourself, whenever you like — see the privacy policy.

Help shape Coastory

Coastory is built by a coaster enthusiast, for coaster enthusiasts — because the apps that already existed didn’t count the way enthusiasts actually count. It’s in private beta right now, which is the good part: you get it early, you find the weird stuff, and you get a say in what happens next.
